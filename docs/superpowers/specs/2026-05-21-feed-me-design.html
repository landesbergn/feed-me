Design Spec — v1.0

Feed Me

A self-serve personal podcast feed of AI-narrated articles · 2026-05-21

Feed Me turns articles you share from iOS into episodes in a private podcast feed. Anyone with the website URL can create their own feed in ~90 seconds — no account, no email, no password. The minimum-viable shape that still lets a friend sign up unassisted: URL-is-account, one canonical iCloud Shortcut, filesystem-as-database. When something fails, you re-share.

1. What we're building (and what we're not)

The job

You read a lot on your phone. You'd listen instead if you could — on the commute, on a dog walk, while cooking. Existing read-it-later-to-audio tools want you to live in their player. Feed Me does the conversion and stops: you listen in Apple Podcasts, Overcast, Pocket Casts, whatever you already use, with the speed controls and queue you've built habits around.

What "done" looks like for v1

  • From any iOS app: Share → Feed Me → an episode appears in your podcast app within ~60 seconds.
  • A friend who opens the website on their phone can be receiving episodes in their podcast app in under two minutes, without ever messaging you for help.
  • The whole system costs under $5/month at hobby scale and you can rebuild it in a weekend.
  • It's a real RSS feed. If anyone ever wants to leave, they take the audio files and the URLs that produced them and go.

What v1 is explicitly not

v1 has no email signup, no magic-link, no passwords, no sessions, no login page, no settings UI beyond a single page rendered at the user's secret URL. There is no in-product visibility into failures, no retries, no paywall fallback, no long-article chunking, no async failure notifications, no episode artwork, no abuse handling, no rate limiting, no admin dashboard.

The single trade-off paying for all of those absences: your URL is your account. Anyone with your /u/<secret> URL can ingest articles into your feed, read it, and change your voice. For "a tool you and your friends use," that's a reasonable security posture (same as the floor's feed-URL model). For "a public service with abuse risk," it isn't — and that's the line between v1 and v2.0 (§5).

3. Self-serve onboarding

~90 seconds, no help required, no account to create. The friction is concentrated in one place: pasting your personal ingest URL into the Shortcut the first time it runs (which iOS makes you do for any user-supplied data — it's the price of admission for share-sheet integrations on iOS).

From landing page to first episode — what a new user actually does
User Types feed-me.app into their iPhone's browser. Lands on a one-screen landing page: "A podcast of the articles you share. Tap below to create your feed — no signup."
Tap Taps Get my feed. Server generates a random secret, creates /data/<secret>/, redirects them to /u/<secret>. That URL is now their account. They're told to bookmark it; the page warns that losing the URL means losing the feed.
Tap From the settings page, tap Install Shortcut. iCloud opens the canonical Feed Me Shortcut; tap Get Shortcut. The Shortcut is the same one every user installs — there's no per-user customization at install time.
Tap Back on the settings page, tap Add to Apple Podcasts (or copy the feed URL into another podcast app). Apple Podcasts opens with their feed and a one-tap Follow button.
User Smoke test: open any article in Safari → Share → Feed Me. The Shortcut runs for the first time and prompts: "Paste your ingest URL." They flip back to the settings page in Safari, tap the Copy ingest URL button, switch back to the Shortcut prompt, paste, hit Done.
Done ~60s later, the article is the newest episode in their podcast app. All subsequent shares are zero-tap-after-the-share-sheet.

Why the "paste URL once" step exists

iOS won't let a website install a Shortcut with custom data baked in. The only way to ship a one-tap Shortcut install is via an iCloud-signed Shortcut, and those are signed once at author time — they can't be generated server-side per user. Every tool that integrates with the iOS Share Sheet (Readwise, Matter, Snipd, AudioPen) hits this same wall, and they all solve it the same way: install a canonical Shortcut, prompt for a token on first run, store it as a Shortcut variable. We do the same, except instead of "an API token" we ask for "your ingest URL" — which is one concept rather than two, and the user already has it on a button they just used.

The settings page (and the only website surface that matters)

One page at /u/<secret>, no JS framework, server-rendered. Renders:

  • The three onboarding buttons (Install Shortcut, Add to Apple Podcasts, Copy ingest URL).
  • A voice picker (radio buttons over shimmer / alloy / nova / echo; submits to POST /u/<secret>/voice).
  • Recent episodes (last 30 rows from the user's directory, including any that failed — shows a one-line error per failure).
  • A "Rotate URL" button — generates a new secret, copies the user's data dir to it, redirects them to the new URL. They re-install the Shortcut (or just edit the saved variable) and re-add the feed in their podcast app. Slow but possible; documented for use only if they suspect their URL leaked.

What we deliberately don't ask the user for

  • Their email. We never need to reach them — there's no password reset, no notifications. Asking for an email creates a privacy obligation we don't want and a signup form they have to fill.
  • A password. The URL is the credential. Passwords would mean a sessions table, bcrypt, login form, password reset flow, 2FA discussion, all of it.
  • A username. The directory on disk is named by the secret. There's no public identity.
  • Confirmation of "yes I bookmarked it." If they didn't, the cost is "create a new feed and re-onboard." Roughly 90 seconds — cheaper than a forced "are you sure you saved this?" interstitial.

4. When things break

What happensHow the user noticesWhat they do
Article extraction fails (paywall, weird layout, JS-rendered) Episode never appears in their podcast app; settings page shows it as failed Re-share from archive.ph, or skip
Article longer than ~4,000 characters Episode appears but cuts off mid-thought Read the rest in Safari, or wait for v1.x chunking
OpenAI rate-limits or has an outage Episode never appears; settings page shows the error Re-share in a few minutes
Server reboots while processing The in-flight article is lost Re-share it
User lost their /u/<secret> bookmark They have no way back to their settings page Create a new feed; the old one orphans and eventually times out (§5). Their old podcast subscription keeps playing old episodes but won't get new ones.
User suspects their URL leaked Strange episodes appear, or paranoia Tap Rotate URL on the settings page; re-install Shortcut + re-subscribe in podcast app (~2 min)
Server runs out of disk on the volume New episodes fail for everyone; admin sees it in Fly metrics Run cleanup (delete oldest MP3s across all users), or resize the volume

The settings page surfaces per-user failures inline, but there are no notifications and no email — if a user shares an article and never opens the settings page, they only learn from absence. That's a deliberate tradeoff (no notification infrastructure in v1); v1.1 (§5) adds optional Pushover/ntfy webhook URLs per user.

5. Out of scope & next steps

Things v1 deliberately doesn't do, sorted by when (and whether) they'd come back. Each item carries the user-experience reason it's deferred, so future-you can re-evaluate honestly instead of cargo-culting.

Will probably come back (v1.x)

v1.1
Optional async failure notifications. Settings page gains a "Notification URL" field; if set, the server POSTs there on any worker exception. Compatible with Pushover, ntfy, Slack webhook, Discord webhook — no email infrastructure required. The user opts in by pasting a URL; we don't host anything.
v1.1
Long-article chunking. The 4,000-char truncation is the most user-visible compromise — every longread hits it. Splitting on paragraph boundaries and concatenating MP3s (with ID3 stripped from chunks 2..N) is ~30 lines and meaningfully changes what's listenable.
v1.2
Wayback paywall fallback. When the direct fetch returns <200 words or hits known paywall phrasing, re-fetch via the Internet Archive Availability API. Recovers maybe 40% of paywalled URLs, ethically. Helpful once users share more NYT / Atlantic / Substack.
v1.2
"Paste text" Shortcut variant. Second Shortcut that POSTs selected text instead of a URL. Catches the long tail of paywalled content not in Wayback.
v1.3
Episode artwork from og:image. Cosmetic, but the generic "FM" tile is ugly in the podcast app once users have a real backlog.
v1.3
Orphan cleanup. Background job: delete /data/<secret>/ directories that haven't had an ingest in 60 days. Reclaims disk from users who tried it once and forgot. Communicated upfront on the landing page: "feeds inactive for 60 days are deleted."

Might come back if the product earns it (v2.0)

v2
Real accounts. Magic-link email signup (Resend), proper sessions, password-less login from any device. Triggered when "I lost my bookmark and want my old feed back" becomes a recurring support request, or when users want to access their feed from multiple devices.
v2
Abuse handling. Per-IP rate limiting on the create-feed endpoint, SSRF allowlist on ingest URLs, captcha or proof-of-work to block automated feed-creation. Required if the URL ever ends up somewhere indexable — until then, security through obscurity holds.
v2
Real persistence. SQLite + a queue table, retries, durable state across restarts, Litestream backup. Necessary when daily traffic is 50+ articles or when "I lost an article" is unacceptable.
v2
Paid tier. Stripe + a usage meter. The Fly + OpenAI bill is small but real; once it's not a personal hobby cost, charging $3/mo (vs OpenAI's actual ~$0.50/user marginal cost) supports the infrastructure.
v2
Branded podcast metadata. Proper podcast cover art per user, owner name, iTunes category, episode-level artwork. The custom domain itself ships in v1 (it's a $12/yr purchase, not a complexity item); the polish around how each user's feed presents inside Apple Podcasts is v2.

Probably never

No
Spotify support. Spotify's consumer app doesn't let listeners subscribe to arbitrary RSS feed URLs, and the workarounds (Spotify for Podcasters with an obscure show name) publicly index the feed. Use Apple Podcasts, Overcast, Pocket Casts, Castro, or AntennaPod.
No
In-app player / Feed Me iOS app. The whole point is to delegate playback to apps that already do it well. Building a player is a different product.
No
Summarization, multi-host, AI commentary. Different product (Snipd, Wondercraft). Feed Me's value is the faithful read — no editorializing.
No
PDF / file uploads. Different ingestion path, larger TTS bills, different audience (academics).
No
Search, transcripts, social sharing, analytics, "for you" recommendations. Each is its own product. Feed Me is a one-way pipe.

6. Implementation notes

Reference material for the build, kept brief and at the end. The product decisions are above; everything here is a means to those ends.

6.1 Architecture

One Fly.io machine (shared-cpu-1x, 256 MB RAM) running a single Python process (FastAPI + Jinja templates). One Fly volume at /data (3 GB) holds all state, namespaced per user:

/data/<secret>/
  settings.json           {voice, created_at, last_ingest_at}
  <slug>.mp3              audio
  <slug>.json             {title, url, ts, error?}

No SQLite, no R2, no worker process, no state machine, no sessions, no cookies. The presence of /data/<secret>/ is what makes a user "exist." Async work runs in daemon threads spawned per ingest request. Failed ingests write a <slug>.json with an error field but no .mp3, so they show up on the settings page as failures.

6.2 HTTP surface

RoutePurpose
GET /Landing page. Static HTML with a single POST /create form.
POST /createGenerate secret = token_urlsafe(32), mkdir /data/<secret>/, write default settings.json, redirect to /u/<secret>.
GET /u/<secret>Settings page. 404 if /data/<secret>/ doesn't exist. Renders the onboarding buttons, voice picker, recent episodes.
POST /u/<secret>/voiceUpdate settings.json. Redirect back to settings.
POST /u/<secret>/rotateGenerate new secret, move the data dir, redirect to new URL.
GET /u/<secret>/ingest?url=…404 if user doesn't exist. Spawn daemon thread; return {"ok": true} in <50 ms.
GET /u/<secret>/feed.xmlList /data/<secret>/*.json (only those with a sibling .mp3), sorted by mtime desc; render RSS 2.0.
GET /u/<secret>/audio/<slug>.mp3Stream the file from /data/<secret>/.
anything else404.

Secrets live in the URL path, not a query string — they stay out of Referer headers and default access-log fields that strip query strings.

6.3 The canonical Shortcut

Authored once by you in the Shortcuts app, shared as an iCloud link, hosted as the Install Shortcut button on every settings page. Five actions:

  1. If dictionary value IngestURL in Shortcut Input is empty
  2.     Ask for Input "Paste your ingest URL from feed-me.app" (URL type)
  3.     Set Variable IngestURL to the provided value (persists across runs via the Shortcut's storage)
  4. End If
  5. URL = {IngestURL}?url={Shortcut Input URL}
  6. Get Contents of URL (GET)
  7. (optional) Show Notification with the response body

In the Shortcut's settings: enable "Show in Share Sheet," restrict accepted types to URLs / Safari Web Pages. The actual persistence mechanism for IngestURL is a Shortcut-level persistent variable (Shortcuts supports these via the "Dictionary" and "Set Variable" actions when scoped correctly — the practical recipe may need a one-line tweak when authored; the design intent is "ask once, remember forever").

6.4 Processing pipeline

  1. httpx.get(url, headers={"User-Agent": "Mozilla/5.0 …"}) — fail on non-2xx.
  2. readability-lxml for title + clean body text.
  3. Truncate body to 4,000 characters.
  4. Read voice from /data/<secret>/settings.json.
  5. openai.audio.speech.create(model="tts-1", voice=voice, input=body) → MP3 bytes.
  6. slug = secrets.token_urlsafe(12); write /data/<secret>/<slug>.mp3 and /data/<secret>/<slug>.json.

Any exception is caught, logged (with the secret elided to the first 6 chars), and written as a failure record: {url, ts, error: "..."} with no sibling .mp3. Settings page reads these and shows them in the "Recent" list. No retries.

6.5 Cost

ItemMonthly
Fly machine (256 MB) + 3 GB volume~$3–4 (fixed)
OpenAI TTS — per active user (~30 articles × ~4 k chars @ $0.60/1 M)~$0.07
Total at 10 active users~$4–5
Total at 50 active users~$7–8

OpenAI is the elastic cost; everything else is roughly fixed at this scale. The economics get uncomfortable around the point where you'd also want abuse handling, rate limits, and a paid tier — which is exactly when v2 makes sense.

6.6 Deploy

fly launch --no-deploy
# edit fly.toml: add [mounts] for /data, 3 GB
fly secrets set OPENAI_API_KEY=sk-...
fly deploy

# Custom domain (one-time):
fly certs add feed-me.app           # prints DNS records to add at registrar
# At your registrar: add the A/AAAA (or CNAME) records Fly prints
fly certs check feed-me.app         # waits for cert issuance, ~minutes

No SECRETS env var; users are created at runtime by POST /create. The only deploy-time secret is the OpenAI key. The custom domain is a one-time setup (~10 min including DNS propagation) and costs ~$12/yr at any registrar.
