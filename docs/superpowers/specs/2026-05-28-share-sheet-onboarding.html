Spec · v1.9

Share Sheet Onboarding

Feed Me · 2026-05-28 · spec

Friends installing Feed Me for the first time can't find the Shortcut in iOS's share sheet — it's buried under a "More" expander and then alphabetically at the bottom of a long list. The user tested this directly and confirmed: the current Share-section instructions on the settings page skip the most important step (tap More with ▼) entirely. This spec rewrites those instructions to mirror what iOS actually does, and adds a one-time "Pin Feed Me" tip that converts every future share into a single tap.

1 · Why

Today's Share section reads:

1. Tap [share icon] at the bottom of the screen — that's the iOS Share button.
2. Scroll to Feed Me and tap it.
3. Wait ~60 seconds.

Step 2 is dead wrong as written. On modern iOS (verified by the user on 2026-05-28), custom Shortcuts don't appear in the default share-sheet view at all. You have to tap a More ▼ button to reveal the full list of actions, then scroll to the bottom (Shortcuts are listed alphabetically, after the built-in actions). Friends who follow the current instructions will tap Share and see no Feed Me, conclude their install is broken, and bounce.

Separately: once a friend completes one share, they're going to want every future share to be one tap instead of "Share → More → scroll → tap". iOS supports pinning a Shortcut to the top of the share sheet via an Edit mode. Most non-technical friends don't know this. A small "Pin Feed Me" tip on the settings page recovers a huge UX win.

2 · What we're building

  1. Revised Share-section copy in templates/settings.html: four steps that mirror what iOS actually does, including the now-required More ▼ tap.
  2. New "Pin Feed Me" tip callout below the steps: small bordered box with 4 sub-steps explaining the pin flow. Visual treatment is friendly (cream background, accent-orange border) so it reads as helpful, not as a warning.
  3. Inline CSS-rendered illustration of the iOS share sheet showing where the More ▼ button sits relative to the app row and the actions. Helps visual learners locate the button without a screenshot.

Pure frontend change. No backend, no new routes, no template variables.

3 · Revised Share-an-article steps

The phase note remains: In Safari (or Mail, Reader, anywhere with a Share button):

Steps (4 instead of 3):

#TitleDescription
1 Tap at the bottom of the screen That's the iOS Share button.
2 Tap More ▼ at the bottom of the share sheet iOS hides Shortcuts behind this. Tapping it reveals the full list of actions.
3 Scroll to the bottom and tap Feed Me Shortcuts are listed alphabetically at the end of the actions. You'll get a "Sent ✓" notification.
4 Wait ~60 seconds The article appears in your podcast app. Pull-to-refresh if it's slow.

The only structural change vs. v1.5 is splitting the old Step 2 into two steps (Tap More, then scroll to Feed Me). The renumbering shifts "Wait" from step 3 to step 4. CSS for the existing 3-row timeline already handles a 4-row layout — no styling work.

4 · New Pin-Feed-Me tip

Placed immediately after the 4 Share steps, before "Recent episodes". A bordered box styled to read as friendly/helpful, not as a warning.

Copy

Title: 💡 Pin Feed Me for one-tap sharing (recommended, one-time)

Body:

  1. Open the share sheet on any article (Share → More)
  2. Scroll to the bottom and tap Edit Actions (or Edit Suggestions on newer iOS)
  3. Find Feed Me in the list, tap the green + next to it
  4. Tap Done. Feed Me is now at the top of your share sheet — one tap from any article forever after.

5 · Inline illustration

Placed between Step 1 and Step 2 of the Share section, OR as a small inline diagram next to Step 2. Shows the iOS share sheet layout with the More ▼ location highlighted.

Implementation: pure CSS-rendered "mockup" — no images. A simplified vertical stack approximating the share sheet: 

┌─────────────────────────────────────┐  ← rounded panel
│  AirDrop  Messages  Mail   ⋯         │  ← apps row
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│  ▢  Copy                             │  ← top actions (a few)
│  ▢  Add to Reading List              │
│  ▢  Find on Page                     │
│  ⌄  More                             │  ← highlighted with arrow callout
└─────────────────────────────────────┘

Implemented as a small <div> with stacked rows, a subtle border, and rounded corners. The "More" row has a bold style + small "tap here" arrow/pointer to make it unmissable. ~80 lines of CSS + markup. Same minimal aesthetic as the rest of the settings page.

Constraints: must look reasonable at the settings-page max-width (480px) on mobile. Renders inside the Share section. Doesn't try to be pixel-accurate to iOS — just conveys the structure and the "More" location.

7 · Testing

Template-only change. Test coverage is via substring assertions in test_settings_renders_for_known_user:

  1. Existing assertions for "Share an article", "Install Shortcut", etc. continue to pass.
  2. New assertion: "More" appears (verifies step 2 has the More ▼ instruction).
  3. New assertion: "Pin Feed Me" appears (verifies the new tip is rendered).

Visual treatment of the callout and the illustration are not testable at the request level. Verified by eye after deploy.

8 · Out of scope

  • Real iOS screenshots. The CSS illustration is good enough and doesn't rot when iOS UI changes (it's intentionally schematic, not literal).
  • Per-iOS-version copy. Older iOS may call the edit button something different. The spec mentions both "Edit Actions" and "Edit Suggestions" inline, so the user can find whichever applies.
  • Detecting whether the user has already pinned. No way to detect from a web page; the tip stays visible always.
  • iOS-version detection / conditional copy. Same text for all iOS versions; trust the user to map between phrasings.
  • Video walkthrough. Was offered, user declined.

9 · Known follow-ups future

  • Replace the CSS illustration with a real screenshot or short video. The CSS schematic is good for v1.9 but a real screenshot would convey the actual look better. Costs maintenance (UI rot) and a screenshot pipeline. Out of v1.9.
  • Auto-pin during install. Apple Shortcuts has no API to programmatically pin a shortcut to the share sheet. If they ever add it, automate this.
  • Detect first-time user vs returning, hide the tip after first share. Would require server-side state and per-user JS — feels heavier than the value. Skip unless friends complain the tip clutters.
